# MIT undergraduate CS reference

Retrieved **2026-11-08**. Start with [the self-study draft](self-study-plan.md).
The earlier [Bilibili shortlist](../mit-bilibili.md) supplies selected videos.

## Sources and scope

- [MIT EECS live 6-3 requirements](https://eecsis.mit.edu/degree_requirements.pcgi?program=6-3):
  labeled **6-3_2026**, applies to students entering in Fall 2026 or later,
  and explicitly includes subjects through Spring 2027. This is the primary
  reference for the requirements below.
- [MIT 6-3 catalog degree chart](https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/computer-science-engineering-course-6-3/):
  a second reference. At retrieval it differed from the live requirements
  in systems alternatives, the C introduction, and additional constraints.
  This document follows the explicitly dated live requirements.
- [Live Course 6 subject catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/6/):
  source for titles, undergraduate/graduate labels, prerequisites, and terms.
- [MIT EECS subject numbering](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/subject-numbering/):
  explains the renumbering that began in Fall 2022.
- [MIT subject listing and schedule](https://student.mit.edu/catalog/index.cgi):
  identifies the current schedule as Fall 2026. A catalog entry alone does
  not establish that a subject is running this term.

This reference covers the **6-3 Computer Science and Engineering major**,
with additional CS, AI, and math choices relevant to self-study. It does not
reproduce the full MIT bachelor's degree: Institute science, humanities,
communication, laboratory, and other requirements need separate treatment.
The self-study sequence is our proposal, not an MIT-prescribed schedule.

## Current 6-3 departmental structure

The following groups summarize the live **6-3_2026** requirements.
Alternatives are choices, not a requirement to take every listed subject.

| Group | Subjects or choices |
| --- | --- |
| Programming preparation | 6.1000, **or** 6.100A + 6.100B, **or** 6.100A + 16.C20 (including its cross-listed equivalents) |
| Low-level programming | 6.1903 **or** 6.1904 — Introduction to Low-level Programming in C and Assembly |
| Discrete mathematics | 6.1200 — Mathematics for Computer Science |
| Additional mathematics | One of 18.05, 18.06, 18.C06/6.C06, 6.3700, or 6.3800 |
| Programming foundation | 6.1010 — Fundamentals of Programming |
| Algorithms foundation | 6.1210 — Introduction to Algorithms |
| Architecture foundation | 6.1910 — Computation Structures |
| Software header | 6.1020 — Software Construction |
| Theory/algorithms header | 6.1220 — Design and Analysis of Algorithms **or** 6.1400 — Computability and Complexity Theory |
| Systems header | One of 6.1800, 6.1810, 6.5660, 6.5830, 6.5831, or 6.5840 |
| Track depth | Two subjects in one CS track; two in a different CS, AI+D, or EE track |
| Further elective | One subject from the permitted EECS, graduate, or mathematics lists |

The live page also specifies at least two advanced undergraduate subjects
from its AUS2/grad_AUS2 lists and two communication-intensive subjects from
its CIM2 list. Exact degree eligibility belongs to MIT's source lists.

## The course inventory

- [Undergraduate CS and AI inventory](undergraduate-cs-subjects.md):
  named subjects grouped by area, with official prerequisite text.
- [Machine-readable snapshot](catalog-snapshot.json): all Course 6 entries
  labeled undergraduate in the retrieved catalog, plus the live CS track
  memberships. The full undergraduate inventory includes electrical
  engineering, special subjects, and non-classroom entries; it is broader
  than a CS major.

Only catalog metadata is extracted; course descriptions are not copied.
The CS-focused inventory uses named subject areas and also includes
undergraduate entries from CS tracks, so cross-cutting and architecture
choices are retained. It does not claim to enumerate every possible
cross-department elective or temporary special-topic course.

Some catalog term fields contain future academic-year dates. They are
preserved as returned by the live catalog, rather than relabeled Fall 2026.
These fields describe catalog scheduling, not public material availability.
